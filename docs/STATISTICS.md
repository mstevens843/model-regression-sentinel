# The statistical method, and what it assumes

This document exists because the brief this project was written against demanded it, and because a
detector whose method is not written down is a detector nobody can argue with. Every choice below is
stated with the alternative it rejected and the assumption it rests on.

The short version: **a raw diff cannot tell provider drift from sampling noise, and this project's
whole technical content is the machinery that can.** Two regimes, because comparing two runs once and
watching a provider forever are different inference problems and conflating them is the single most
common way a drift monitor fails.

---

## Why a diff is not enough

At temperature above zero a model is a distribution, not a function. Two runs of an identical corpus
against an identical model string differ, and the size of that difference is not small relative to
the drift anyone is trying to catch.

Measured on this machine, n=8 replicates per condition, alias `sonnet`, default temperature:

| condition | metric | median | CV | max / median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| constrained one-word answer | latency | 1385 ms | 7.5% | 1.06x |
| constrained numeric answer | latency | 1499 ms | 12.6% | 1.09x |
| free-form one sentence | latency | 2275 ms | **70.8%** | **3.57x** |
| free-form one sentence | output tokens | 71 | 18.5% | 1.32x |
| structured JSON with reasoning | output tokens | 1062 | ~32% | 1.56x |

Those are four different noise regimes, not four samples of one. A detector tuned on the first row
and shipped against the third will cry regression on ordinary provider behavior; a threshold loose
enough for the third will miss real drift in the first. This is why `Archetype` is part of the case
schema and why the corpus is required to span all four.

## Regime A: comparing two runs

Command: `sentinel compare`. Implementation: `packages/detect/src/compare.ts`.

### The unit of analysis is the case, not the replicate

Cases differ enormously in difficulty. In this corpus several return the same token on every draw
and some are genuinely ambiguous. Pooling replicates across cases lets that variation dominate, and
a test that spends its power on the fact that case 3 is harder than case 7 has none left for the
question anyone asked. Pairing by case removes case difficulty entirely.

### Primary test: a paired sign-flip permutation test

For case *i*, the statistic is `d_i = rate_candidate(i) - rate_baseline(i)`, and the suite statistic
is the mean of those over cases. The null is that the `d_i` are symmetric about zero, and the null
distribution is generated by flipping their signs, which is exactly the symmetry the null asserts.

- **Exact up to 20 cases.** All `2^k` sign assignments are enumerated, so the p-value is exact
  rather than estimated. Above that the null is sampled with a seeded generator, and the count
  includes the observed assignment: `(count + 1) / (draws + 1)`. Omitting that correction permits a
  reported p-value of zero, which is a statement no sample can support.
- **Assumption:** under no drift the sign of each per-case delta is exchangeable. This holds when
  the two arms have the same number of replicates, because then the two rate estimates have equal
  sampling variance and their difference is symmetric about zero. **When the arms differ in size the
  test is approximate**, the result carries `exchangeable: false`, and every report prints it.
- **Rejected: a t-test.** There is no defensible normality assumption over `k` bounded proportions,
  several of which sit exactly at 0 or 1. In the pilot two constrained cases returned 8/8 identical
  answers, so their per-case rate was exactly 1 with exactly zero observed variance. A t-test on a
  sample containing those is not conservative, it is undefined.

### Second test: the empirically calibrated null

A textbook null assumes the only randomness is sampling from a fixed distribution. A provider is not
that. Temperature nondeterminism, routing, batching, load, and whatever the vendor changed this
morning all contribute variation no binomial model knows about.

So the baseline describes its own noise. Its replicates are split into two random halves, the same
suite statistic is computed on that split, and the split is repeated 500 times. That is the
distribution of the statistic under a condition where drift is known to be absent, because both
halves came from one provider in one window against one corpus. The candidate is scored against that
measured distribution.

- **It is conservative, and here is why.** Splitting `n` replicates in half gives each A/A arm `n/2`,
  so the calibration null is the statistic's distribution at HALF the sample size the real comparison
  uses. It is therefore more variable than the real one, its quantiles sit wider, and the calibrated
  p-value is biased toward saying no drift. Erring toward silence is the right direction for a tool
  whose main failure mode is crying wolf.
- **Assumption:** replicates within the baseline are exchangeable. They are collected in one window
  against one provider, so this is close to true, and it is closer to true than the alternative
  assumption that they are draws from a known parametric family.
- **A finding must clear both nulls.** When they disagree that is information rather than a problem:
  a permutation p of 0.01 beside a calibrated p of 0.30 means the effect is real relative to sampling
  and utterly ordinary for this provider. That is precisely the case where a diff-based tool declares
  a regression and is wrong.

### Effect size, always beside the p-value

A seeded percentile bootstrap over cases, 2000 resamples. The p-value says something moved; the
interval says by how much, plausibly. Only the second is actionable, and a report that prints a
p-value without an interval invites a reader to treat significance as magnitude.

### Continuous metrics are compared in relative terms

For token counts and latency, the per-case difference is expressed relative to that case's own
scale. One case in this corpus averages about 76 output tokens and another about 1190; an unweighted
mean of raw differences across those is a statistic about the second case wearing the name of the
suite.

**The statistic is the SYMMETRIC percent difference, `2(c-b)/(c+b)`, and not `(c-b)/b`.** This
document described the naive ratio for a full release after the code had stopped using it, which is
its own small lesson. Dividing by the baseline is unbounded, and this corpus contains a case that
breaks it: `cnt-c-003` is bimodal, running 5 to 480 output tokens in the recorded baseline, so a
half whose draws land on the short mode produces a ratio in the thousands of percent and that one
case dominates a statistic that averages over cases. The symmetric form agrees with the naive one to
first order for the small changes that matter and is bounded in [-2, 2], so no single case can swamp
the suite. `packages/detect/src/stats.ts` carries the measurement.

### Per-case screening is triage, and is labelled as such

Fisher exact for binary metrics, Mann-Whitney U for continuous, then **Benjamini-Hochberg at
q = 0.10** across cases.

- **Why FDR and not Bonferroni.** The question is which cases deserve a human's attention, not which
  are proven to have moved. Bonferroni controls the probability of any false positive at all, which
  across 24 cases and several metrics leaves essentially no power at these sample sizes, and a screen
  that never fires is a screen nobody reads. FDR controls the expected proportion of false positives
  among those flagged, which is the quantity that matters when someone is deciding what to look at.
- **Assumption:** the case-level tests are independent or positively dependent. Cases are separate
  prompts scored by separate graders, so independence is a fair approximation, and the dependence
  that does exist - one provider, one time window - is positive, which is the direction BH tolerates.
  Benjamini-Yekutieli would hold under arbitrary dependence at the cost of a log factor in power. It
  is not used, and this sentence is why.
- **The floor that makes this triage rather than evidence.** With 10 replicates per arm, the smallest
  attainable two-sided Fisher p is reached only at a perfect 10/10 against 0/10 split. A per-case
  test cannot reach significance on anything subtler than total collapse. Reports say this.

### Mann-Whitney is exact where it can be

The exact null distribution is enumerated by dynamic programming when there are no ties and the
table is small (`n1 * n2 <= 2500` and `n1 + n2 <= 60`), which covers every replicate count this
project uses. With ties the exact distribution is not valid and a tie-corrected normal approximation
with a continuity correction is used instead. **Which one ran is reported**, because they are
different claims. The arrangement counts are asserted against the binomial coefficient in the tests,
because a recurrence that has drifted still sums to something and checking it sums to the right thing
is the cheap way to notice.

### The location estimate is Hodges-Lehmann, not a difference of means

The median of all pairwise differences. Robust to the fat right tail every latency sample has. In the
pilot one sample in eight was 3.57 times the median, and it would have moved a mean by more than any
drift this tool is looking for.

## Power, and what a null result is worth

**This is the part that separates a drift detector from a diff.** A comparison that finds nothing has
two possible meanings, and they are opposites: nothing moved, or the instrument cannot see a movement
of this size. Reporting the first when the truth is the second converts "we did not look hard enough"
into "we checked".

### The minimum detectable effect is simulated, not quoted

A closed-form power calculation needs one per-case variance, and this corpus does not have one: some
cases returned 8/8 identical answers and a free-form case had an output-token CV of 18.5 percent. A
formula fed the average of those describes no case in the corpus. So the simulator draws from the
observed per-case baseline rates, at the actual replicate count, and runs the actual test including
its permutation step. What comes back is the power of this suite rather than of an idealized one.

- **The alternative is a uniform drop across every case.** A provider update that degrades everything
  a little is both the most plausible drift shape and the hardest to see, because no single case
  collapses to point at. A detector tuned on one case falling off a cliff would report a far better
  MDE than it deserves.
- **The zero-variance trap is handled explicitly.** A case observed at 10/10 has a point estimate of
  exactly 1, and simulating from p = 1 gives a case that can never fail. Rates are shrunk toward one
  half by the Jeffreys prior, `(successes + 0.5) / (n + 1)`, which at n = 10 turns 1.000 into 0.955.

### The rule of three

With zero failures in `n` trials, the 95 percent upper bound on the true failure rate is about `3/n`.

| replicates | an all-passing arm is still consistent with a true failure rate up to |
|---|---|
| 5 | 60.0% |
| 8 | 37.5% |
| **10** | **30.0%** |
| 20 | 15.0% |
| 30 | 10.0% |
| 50 | 6.0% |
| 100 | 3.0% |

At the ten replicates this project ships, a case that passes every single draw is consistent with a
true failure rate of thirty percent. That number is printed in every `INCONCLUSIVE` report. It is the
reason "all green" is weak evidence and the most useful honest thing this tool says.

### When the effect is below the MDE, the verdict is INCONCLUSIVE

Not `NO_DRIFT`. The report prints the replicate count that would reach the effect the user says they
care about.

## Regime B: watching continuously

Command: `sentinel watch --tick`. Implementation: `packages/detect/src/eprocess.ts`.

### The peeking problem is the whole reason this is a separate regime

A fixed-alpha test is valid once. Run it hourly at alpha = 0.05 and, under a null where nothing is
changing at all, it fires about once every twenty hours. Every one of those is someone investigating
a provider that did not change, and after the second or third the alerting gets switched off.

This does not arise for promptfoo or any other pre-merge eval tool, because they are run once per
change by construction. It arises here because the product is continuous.

### The fix is a test martingale (an e-process)

Wealth starts at 1 and is bet on each observation. Under the null the wealth process is a
non-negative martingale, so Ville's inequality bounds the probability it EVER exceeds `1/alpha`, at
any stopping time, over an unbounded number of looks:

```
P( sup_t  W_t  >=  1/alpha )  <=  alpha
```

So "wealth crossed 20" is a valid alpha = 0.05 rejection however often it was checked. No alpha
spending schedule, no penalty for looking, and nothing has to be decided in advance about how long
the watch will run.

- **The bet.** Wealth is multiplied by `1 + lambda * (p0 - X)` on each observation; under the null
  that factor has expectation 1. `lambda` is Kelly-optimal for a named alternative,
  `lambda* = (p0 - p1) / (p0 * (1 - p0))`, so the watcher is tuned by saying what size of regression
  matters rather than by choosing a statistical constant.
- **`p0` is a Wilson lower confidence bound, not the point estimate.** A baseline of 10/10 has a
  point estimate of 1.0, and betting against 1.0 treats a single failure as infinitely surprising.
  The lower bound requires more evidence, which is the correct direction.
- **`lambda` is clamped to 90 percent of its maximum.** At the boundary a single observation drives
  wealth to exactly zero, and zero is absorbing: a real regression arriving later would be
  undetectable forever.
- **Assumption:** observations are independent given no drift, and `p0` is known well enough. The
  first is the weakest assumption in this document. Provider behavior is plausibly autocorrelated in
  time - a deploy, a busy hour, a regional rollout - and positive autocorrelation makes the wealth
  process less conservative than the bound suggests. Using a lower confidence bound for `p0` buys
  back some of that margin. It is not a proof and this paragraph is not a substitute for one.

### CUSUM sits beside it, never instead of it

The e-process says whether something happened with a guarantee attached. It does not say when it
started, which is usually the first question a person asks. CUSUM answers that and carries no such
guarantee, so it is reported as an aid to reading and never as a trigger.

### Confirmation is required before anything fails a build

An alarm alone returns exit code 0. A crossing must reproduce on an independently collected run
before the verdict becomes `CONFIRMED_DRIFT` and the exit code becomes 1. Two tests at alpha is a far
stronger guard than moving alpha, and it is one a person can reason about without a textbook.

## What is measured about the detector itself

A detector that has never been shown to control its own false-positive rate is a claim, not a tool.

1. **A suite of calibration scenarios** (`packages/detect/src/scenarios.ts`), covering A/A
   quietness, detection of a real effect, refusal to overclaim a tiny one, latency non-gating, the
   peeking problem, the replicate floor, corpus mismatch, confirmation, identity change, spent
   sensitivity, metadata drift, and a collection that failed outright.
2. **A pool of detector mutants** (`packages/detect/src/mutants/`), each a plausible wrong detector
   with a `mustFail` list, enforced by `test/suiteDiscriminates.test.ts`.

   Counts are deliberately not typed here. They were, and they went stale: this document said ten
   and eight while the repository held thirteen and eleven. `RESULTS.md` carries the generated
   table, regenerated by `pnpm controls` from a run of every mutant against every scenario.
3. **The anti-vacuity argument.** Almost every honesty property of a drift detector has the form
   "does not report drift when there is none", and every one of them is satisfied by a detector that
   never reports anything. `alwaysQuiet` is shipped specifically so that cannot quietly become true
   of the real detector, and its `mustFail` list is exactly the scenarios that require a detection.

See `results/CALIBRATION.md` for the measured numbers.

## Assumptions, collected in one place

| assumption | where it bites | how it is mitigated |
|---|---|---|
| Per-case delta signs are exchangeable under the null | the permutation p-value | holds exactly when arms have equal replicates; otherwise the result is marked approximate |
| Baseline replicates are exchangeable | the calibrated null | collected in one window against one provider |
| Case-level tests are independent or positively dependent | Benjamini-Hochberg | cases are separate prompts with separate graders; BH tolerates positive dependence |
| Observations are independent given no drift | the e-process guarantee | a conservative `p0` buys margin; autocorrelation is a real and unquantified risk |
| A uniform drop is a realistic drift shape | the MDE | it is the hardest shape to detect, so the MDE is conservative |
| The graders measure what the case intends | everything | graders are deterministic code, and `requiredSignals` ties a case to the metric it is about |
| The provider reports its identity honestly | the fingerprint | not verifiable from outside; a vendor can re-tag identical weights, which the report states |

## What no amount of statistics fixes

- **Deterministic graders cannot score everything.** An LLM judge is itself a drifting instrument, so
  it is not used. Drift in a judgement no code can grade is invisible to this tool, and the cases
  where that is true carry a `detectionLimit` and are counted in their own row.
- **A baseline ages.** Latency and cost comparisons across an old baseline are observational, and
  `packages/baseline` degrades their trust rather than reporting them as if they were current.
- **Correlation with a cause is not established.** This tool detects that behavior moved. It does not
  and cannot establish that a provider changed a model, as opposed to changing routing, capacity,
  a safety filter, or something nobody outside the vendor can name.
